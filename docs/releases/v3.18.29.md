# v3.18.29 — Root causes behind the dead ends: cards you answer in words, provider answers that count, and a faster road to the brain

Mac-only release. Harness schema stays at 93 and memory schema at 37; no
database migration. Everything below was found by running the installed app
against live models on 2026-10-06 and 2026-10-07, fixed at the framework
level, hotpatched into the installed app (patches 22–50 over v3.18.28) and
re-run live; the suite results are recorded as measured.

## What changed

### Approval cards you answer in words

- A queued command or send has one card, written by Clem: what she needs to
  do and why, the exact command or content under it, "Yes, go ahead" / "No,
  skip it". The chat line, the Needs-you list and the phone title all carry
  her words. Approving runs it once; the ending shows what it printed, and
  the brain finishes the rest of the request from that result.
- A typed "Yes, delete it." when Jev cannot read the reply (unavailable, or
  past its deadline under load) no longer starts a fresh turn. That fresh
  turn used to branch away from the waiting card and mint a duplicate card.
  Clem now asks the card's own question back, once ("Just to be sure —
  should I …?"); a plain yes or no to it is parsed exactly, with no model
  call. The phone does the same. The Jev deadline for approval replies is
  4 s, a worst-case wait on a typed decision rather than a failure.
- An approved card whose stored action can never activate ends visibly
  once, instead of re-failing on every boot.
- Deletes always ask. A learned kind skips the card only for the change you
  asked for.

### Provider answers count as answers

- A delete of something already gone, a send to an address the provider
  rejects: the provider processed the call and said no, and nothing changed.
  That refusal is now evidence, not an "unrecovered failure". The settlement
  audit stops asking the delivery judge about it, the ledger records it as a
  failed write, and the completion reviewer reads an end state that already
  holds as done. Live, the old path ended with Clem asking the owner to
  "check your Slack reminders list by hand" after she had already verified
  the list was empty.
- A provider refusal is bound to the exact request. The same call again is
  the same refusal (the loop-breaker still stops it); a repaired call is a
  new request the brain may answer. When the governor does stop on a
  provider refusal, the check-in delivers the provider's answer in Clem's
  words instead of asking a question that invites workarounds. Live, the old
  check-in asked whether to try "another workspace", the owner said yes, and
  the next turn chased a Slack account that does not exist.
- A reply the terminal delivery judge rewrote and chose to deliver is
  vouched for by the completion verdict of the account it rendered. Every
  judge-delivered turn used to publish as "unverified … recorded against
  different reply text" under the right words.
- An empty collection with no continuation is the whole of it, even when the
  provider never said so; an operation the provider lists but will not
  define points the brain at served siblings, never at itself.

### No way around the gate

- A connected-app call made through the shell is a network mutation,
  whoever ran it: the provider's own CLI (`composio execute`, `composio
  proxy … -X POST`) and any command that sends a mutating request to a
  remote URL, even through a shell variable or a full path. Live, the brain
  found the Composio CLI and deleted and created Slack reminders through it
  as "compute": no card, no ledger, no learned kind.

### Faster road to the brain

- The effect judge and the whole-plan grounding judge run together on an
  admitted write. In series under load they cost 12 s + 30 s before a 13 s
  proposal was even repaired: 96 s before the turn started.
- A reply the quick interpretation could not admit reaches the brain after
  one proposer deadline, not two (20 s saved under load).
- A `continue_goal` that restates the goal is read as the amendment it is,
  before any model repair is spent. Live, "Delete that same reminder again,
  with the same id, and tell me what happened" was refused as an illegal
  payload and the owner was told "That is a new request; I am not executing
  or approving it here".
- A card row whose definition the provider re-versioned (Composio stamps
  definitions by date) reopens the frozen planning card instead of refusing
  the whole turn as "capability drifted". Live, a typed yes failed in 3 s
  with "I couldn't finish this request. The error's cause is not confirmed
  yet."
- The delivery judge's one-resume cap survives across activations; nine
  judge rounds no longer burn 223 s on one turn.
- The judge's question never carries the host's retained-work inventory.

## Live evidence (installed app, live home)

29 guarded daemon-only hotpatches on 2026-10-06/07, scenario waves 22–26
against DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (brain), claude-sonnet-5 (reviewer) and Jev
1.13.0, all recorded under `~/clem-worktrees/takeover-kit/live/`. On the
final code:

- Slack reminder scenario, six turns, all done or asked as designed: create;
  "Yes, set it." does not duplicate; "Now delete that reminder" → one card;
  "Yes, delete it." → "Slack returned not_found … nothing there to delete"
  (done, 12 s); "Delete that same reminder again" → one card; "Yes, go
  ahead." → done, honest. Whole scenario 176 s (was 280 s before the
  parallel judges).
- Write a file → one card → "Yes, go ahead." runs it → "Continue." finishes.
- Outlook draft created on a learned kind with no card; "Now delete that
  draft" → one card → "Yes, delete it." → deleted and read back.

## Known limits

- A genuinely new instruction typed while a card is waiting still branches
  into a successor conversation and leaves the card behind; carrying or
  closing that card is next.
- The pre-turn semantic lane still costs 30–60 s on a loaded machine with a
  slow brain (one proposal plus two judges); the judges now run together.
- Slack's `reminders.list` returns an empty list for reminders created
  through the API, so Clem can only confirm a reminder through Slack's own
  answer to a delete.
