# v3.18.44 — What you approve runs; the account you named stays named; the chat fills the window

**No database migration** (harness schema stays 95), so this updates in place
over 3.18.43. The Mac build comes from the release workflow (signed and
notarized). The Windows x64 build of the same commit is qualified by the
private Windows pipeline from this tag, **unsigned** until a code-signing
certificate exists.

## What you approve on a card runs

- **An approved write is no longer refused as a "duplicate".** Clem's
  duplicate check is meant to stop the same thing going out twice, and a card
  you approve after the earlier write is your say-so to send it. That
  approval was never being counted for an action Clem had prepared and asked
  about, or for a command-line write. So after Clem marked one record
  complete, creating a follow-up in the same account was refused even after
  you approved it. Your approval now counts, once, for exactly what the card
  showed.

## The account you named stays named

- **Naming the account once is enough, even in a long chat.** Clem checks
  that the account she uses is the one you asked for. In a long conversation
  that check could give up, so she kept asking "which account?" after you had
  answered, and a read she needed could not run. The check now looks only at
  what changed since it last confirmed the account, and the account named in
  your current message is checked on those words.
- **Accounts are offered by their names.** Clem no longer shows an account as
  "default send account" (her own bookmark for it) or with a connection id;
  she uses the name you gave it, or its label or email.

## The chat fills the window

- On a wide screen the conversation column grows with the window instead of
  sitting in a narrow strip.

## Reliability

- The phone relay stops cleanly when it is asked to stop while starting up.
- When a memory change Clem made cannot be confirmed, she says so as her own
  question rather than an extra reply that was discarded.

## Known issues

- When Clem plans a change made through a command-line tool, the plan lists it
  as local work; the change still asks you before it leaves this computer.
- Saving a memory can take over a minute when the check disputes how Clem
  worded her confirmation.
- The Windows build is unsigned.
